# Exercise selection method - what earns a slot in the rebuilt routine

**Written 2026-09-03.** Research pass requested by Daniel for the whole-week reorganisation that is
parked until his uni timetable lands. This is the **menu and the decision rules, not the finished
week** - day assignments stay frozen, and every structural change here is a proposal for Daniel to
approve.

Read alongside [exercise-notes.md](exercise-notes.md), which holds what they have each said about
the exercises they already do. This file answers a different question: given their circumstances,
which exercises deserve a place at all.

## The one thing in this file

**Time is the binding constraint, not knowledge.** Their sessions run 66-90 minutes, both of them
have cut sessions short repeatedly, and from 7 September Daniel works Saturdays and Sundays. So an
exercise is not judged on whether it is good. It is judged on **return per minute against a stated
goal** - and the biggest single win available is not cutting exercises at all, it is pairing them.

---

# Part 1 - The selection rules

## 1. Every exercise names the goal it serves

The stated goals, and nothing else counts as one:

| Person | Goals |
|---|---|
| Daniel | 100 kg bench, 200 kg squat, 200 kg deadlift (multi-year), sub-20 5k |
| Cerys | First unassisted pull-up |
| Both | Hyrox mixed doubles |

An exercise that cannot be traced to one of these is the first thing to go when the session
overruns - which it will, because it always has. Note this is **not** designing around an assumption
that work gets skipped, which is a standing override. It is the opposite: fixing the session's
requirements so the written work fits, which is what that override actually asks for, and what
Daniel's own backlog item means by *shorten the sessions before adding anything to the week*.

## 2. They are doing far more sets than the minimum, so cut sets before cutting exercises

- **Strength** needs **2-3 working sets per exercise per week**, close to failure, at 70-85% 1RM and
  6-12 reps. For time-limited trainees even a single hard set of 6-12 reps at 70-85%, 2-3 times a
  week for 8-12 weeks, produces significant strength gains.
- **Hypertrophy** needs **4 working sets per muscle group per week**, close to failure, at 6-15 reps.
- Strength shows **more pronounced diminishing returns to volume** than hypertrophy does.

Both upper sessions currently carry **eight exercises**, and so does Lower 2. Against those numbers
that is well past the minimum, and Daniel has twice asked to cut sets for time. Cutting a set costs
almost nothing; cutting an exercise costs a movement pattern.

## 3. The structural fix is pairing, not deletion

This is the highest-value finding of the pass. **Antagonist paired sets (supersets) cut session
duration by roughly half** - in one study the traditional session ran 23 minutes, or 66%, longer
than the superset version - **without compromising total volume, muscle activation, perceived
recovery, or chronic adaptations in maximal strength, strength endurance or hypertrophy.** Pairing
agonist with antagonist also lets you complete *more* reps than straight sets.

Two caveats, stated plainly. Supersets produce **higher internal load, more muscle damage and higher
perceived exertion**, so they may need longer between sessions - which matters for a pair who both
already report fatigue. And rest interval matters: **self-selected or 120 seconds** beat both 60 and
180 seconds for balancing volume against efficiency.

Obvious pairings in their current program: bench with a row, pulldown with a press, leg curl with
leg extension, triceps with biceps. If a rebuilt Upper 1 paired four of its eight exercises, it
would plausibly finish inside an hour without losing a single movement.

## 4. Prefer exercises that serve two goals at once

Under a time constraint, an exercise that pays into two goals beats two exercises that each pay into
one. Part 2 is ordered on exactly this.

## 5. Keep running and lifting apart

The interference effect - blunted strength and hypertrophy when endurance work is trained alongside
- is real but manageable, and the management is scheduling:

- Interference is **worst when the two are in the same session** (20 minutes apart or less) and much
  reduced when separated by at least 3 hours.
- Aim for **different days**; where that is impossible, **6 hours minimum**, ideally 6-24.
- **Non-consecutive days** prevent the attenuation entirely.
- Cap endurance work at **no more than 3 days a week**.
- **Running interferes more than cycling does.** Where a session is purely aerobic and the shins are
  the concern anyway, the bike is the cheaper choice - which is what they both reached for
  independently as a warm-up on 20 August.

## 6. Name discipline

Any new exercise gets its own name and keeps it. Records, the Last column and the progress chart all
key on the name, so a rename orphans history and a reused name splices two different movements into
one trend. See the exercise-name fork item in [BACKLOG.md](BACKLOG.md), which is already biting.

# Part 2 - The pool

## A. Serves two or more goals - take these first

### Sled push and sled pull
**The standout of this research pass**, and they already own a sled.

- It is **two of the eight Hyrox stations**, trainable directly and as written.
- It is **concentric-only with minimal eccentric action**, which means **markedly less muscle damage
  and DOMS** than equivalent lifting. That allows more volume and better recovery between muscle
  groups while still producing performance gains, and makes it a standard choice in rehab and injury
  prevention.
- It improves sprint performance, power output and acceleration.

Why that combination matters here: **Cerys's limiter is tissue tolerance.** A hard lower-body
stimulus that does not damage tissue is precisely what her situation asks for, and it is the same
exercise that trains a station she has to perform on race day. Nothing else in this file does both.

### Farmers carry
Also a Hyrox station, and it solves a co-limiter rather than adding a new demand.

- **Grip endurance is the co-limiter on the sled pull** ([hyrox-method.md](hyrox-method.md)), and
  grip is typically what fails in deadlift training before the legs or back do. Daniel is chasing a
  200 kg deadlift.
- The trunk works isometrically throughout - erectors, rectus abdominis, obliques - so it does the
  job their dedicated core exercises are doing, in the same minutes as the station practice.
- **Programming:** 20-40 m or 20-30 seconds per set for strength; 40-60 seconds for conditioning.

### Romanian deadlift
The single **highest-carryover deadlift accessory**: heavy hamstring work plus isometric upper-back
and lat strength. It pays into Daniel's deadlift, into the posterior chain that running economy
depends on, and into the sandbag lunges station. One exercise, three goals.

### Barbell row
Lats, traps and rhomboids stabilise the bar in **both the squat and the bench**, so it serves two of
Daniel's three barbell goals at once, and it is a horizontal pull that also feeds Cerys's pull-up.

### Incline walk
Already programmed for Cerys and already proven at HR ~139 with no shin pain. It is her aerobic
engine at zero tissue cost. Do not treat it as a consolation exercise; it is load-bearing in the
plan and should survive the rebuild untouched.

## B. Serves one goal, but is the best available at it

### Pull-up eccentrics, for Cerys
Her only stated strength goal, and the backlog already carries this.

- **Negatives progress fastest and transfer best** of the three common methods, because eccentric
  contractions let you load beyond what you can currently lift, and eccentric training produces
  greater strength adaptations than concentric-only.
- **Bands** reduce load at the bottom, where most of the difficulty sits, and their variable
  resistance matches the strict pull-up's strength curve.
- **The combination beats either alone.** Eccentrics are the foundation; the band keeps her training
  the whole movement.

### Close-grip bench press, for Daniel
For bench lockout. Chosen over skullcrushers deliberately: **both of them have flagged the triceps
pushdown** ([exercise-notes.md](exercise-notes.md)) - her shoulder, his elbow - so a pressing pattern
is the joint-friendlier route to the same muscle.

### Front squat, paused squat, tempo squat
Squat accessories chosen by weakness rather than by default. Paused and tempo work targets staying
tight out of the bottom; front squats target quads and the upright position. Cerys stops above
parallel, so tempo and paused work at a depth she can hold is the honest starting point rather than
loading a range she cannot yet reach.

## C. Shin-safe loading for Cerys

Shins are the governor and twelve days of walking-only fixed a three-week problem, so this category
is about keeping the tibia quiet while still training.

- **Strengthen the calves, tibialis anterior and intrinsic foot muscles** - this reduces the strain
  placed on the shinbone during activity, and dorsiflexor strength plus ankle coordination is what
  holds MTSS at bay reliably.
- Practical exercises: **heel raises** (she has `Leg press calf raise` already), **toe walks**, and
  **resisted dorsiflexion**. The dorsiflexion work is the gap - nothing in the current program trains
  the front of the shin at all.
- Hip, calf and core strengthening all feature in the standard prescription, which is convenient
  given her hip history.
- **Never increase running more than 10% a week.**

## D. What to cut, and the honest reason

None of these is a bad exercise. Each is simply the cheapest thing to lose when the session has to
get shorter, judged against rule 1.

| Exercise | Where | Why it goes first |
|---|---|---|
| **Bicep curl / Hammer curl** | Upper 1, Upper 2 | Pure isolation, traceable to no stated goal. Rows and pull-ups already load the elbow flexors |
| **Lateral raise** | Upper 1, Upper 2 | Isolation, twice a week, no stated goal. Cerys has an open form flag on it. Candidate replacement is the Lu raise - see [exercise-notes.md](exercise-notes.md) section F |
| **Cable crossover** | Upper 2 | Redundant against two pressing exercises in the same session |
| **Leg extension** | Lower 1 | Same category as the leg press Daniel has already proposed dropping |
| **Hip abduction / Hip adduction** | Lower 2 | Six flags from Cerys in six weeks. Band and cable versions do the same job standing |

That is potentially five to seven exercises out of the two upper sessions and Lower 2, which is
roughly the time Daniel has been trying to reclaim by cutting sets.

# Part 3 - The gap nothing in the program addresses

**Compromised running - running immediately after station work - does not exist anywhere in their
week.** In doubles the running is 60-65% of the race, and the whole difficulty of the event is
running well on legs that have just done a sled push. Station practice in isolation does not train
it, and neither does a standalone run.

This cannot be solved by swapping an exercise, because it is a session type rather than an exercise.
It needs its own slot, which collides directly with the overrun problem, and that collision is the
main thing the rebuild has to resolve. Flagging it here rather than proposing a shape, because the
shape depends on the timetable Daniel does not have yet.

# Part 4 - Open questions for Daniel

1. **Are supersets acceptable?** They are the cheapest way to keep every exercise and still finish
   an hour earlier, but they raise perceived exertion and may need more recovery between sessions.
   Worth trying on one upper session before committing the whole week.
2. **Does the sled go in?** It is the best-value exercise available to them and it needs a slot.
3. **How much of section D goes?** Cutting all of it is aggressive; cutting the curls and one
   lateral raise slot is the conservative version.
4. **Where does compromised running live**, if anywhere, before the race is actually booked?
