# Training week method - where things go, and why the last session keeps disappearing

**Written 2026-09-02.**

This file exists for two reasons.

The first is that the biggest finding of the 2 September research pass - **a second run per week** -
has nowhere obvious to go, and "just add a run" is not a plan when five days are already full.

The second is that Daniel wrote this on 28 August:

> "new work schedule comes starting the 7th september - when the schedule lines up properly"

**The week is about to be rebuilt anyway.** That makes this the right moment to decide what the
week is for, rather than shuffling the existing one.

---

| Day | Session | Exercises |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Lower 2 | 8 - Squat, seated leg curl, walking/sandbag lunges, hip abduction, hip adduction, leg press calf raise, back extension, goblet squat |
| **Tuesday** | Upper 1 | 8 - Bench press, pull-ups, lat pulldown, incline DB press, seated cable row, triceps pushdown, lateral raise, bicep curl |

| **Friday** | **Lower 1** | 6 - **Deadlift**, leg press, leg extension, seated leg curl, leg press calf raise, back extension |
| Optional | Cardio: Endurance + Core | 5 |
| Optional | Weekend run | 1 |
| Sat, Sun | off | |

**The design is good.** Judged against the sequencing rules in Part 4, it scores well: the run sits
on Wednesday, two days after Lower 2 and two days before Lower 1, so the hardest cardio is nowhere
near either heavy leg day. The two upper days flank the run and load different tissue. Nobody needs
to redesign this on principle.

# Part 2 - The week as actually executed, and the thing it explains

Map the logged sessions onto real weekdays and a pattern appears immediately:

| Date | Day | Session | Programmed for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 Aug | Tuesday | Lower 2 | Monday |
| 19 Aug | Wednesday | Upper 1 | Tuesday |
| 20 Aug | Thursday | Run: Daniel | Wednesday |
| 21 Aug | Friday | Upper 2 | Thursday |
| 26 Aug | Wednesday | Run: Daniel | Wednesday |
| 28 Aug | Friday | Lower 1 | Friday |
| 29 Aug | Saturday | Upper 2 | Thursday |

**The whole week runs about one day late, and the drift is not constant - it slips and catches up.**

## What that costs, specifically

**Lower 1 is last in the week, so Lower 1 is the session that falls off the end.** It has been logged
**twice since 20 July**: once on 20 July and once on 28 August.

That single fact explains something `strength-method.md` Part 1 flagged as a mystery: **Daniel has
exactly one deadlift top set on record, ever.** The obvious readings were that the deadlift is a
weak point, or that it is being avoided. Neither is right.

**The deadlift is not untrained because of anything to do with the deadlift. It is untrained because
it is the first exercise of the session that sits last in a week that keeps slipping.** The 28
August note confirms the session is taken seriously when it happens:

> "only deadlift - focused on form and getting the right weight for next session"

That is a deliberate, sensible feeler session, not avoidance.

## The design principle that falls out of it

> **Whatever sits last in the week is the thing you will lose.** A five-day week that starts a day
> late becomes a four-day week, and it is always the same day that goes.

So the position of a session in the week is a statement about its priority whether or not anyone
intended it that way. Two ways to act on that:

1. **Put the most important session earliest**, so drift eats the least important one.
2. **Make the week robust to slipping** - which usually means fewer, shorter sessions rather than a
   tighter schedule.

Right now the deadlift, one of three named strength goals, is in the position reserved for the thing
you are willing to lose.

# Part 3 - The real constraint is session length, not the number of days

This is the part that changes what "adding a run" means.

## The durations

| Session | Duration |
|---|---|
| Lower 2, 18 Aug | **92 min** |
| Lower 2, 11 Aug | **88 min** |
| Upper 1, 12 Aug | 73 min |
| Upper 1, 19 Aug | 66 min |
| Upper 2, 29 Aug | 65 min |
| Lower 1, 20 Jul | 75 min |

## What Daniel keeps writing

Straight from the session feedback, over six weeks:

- "cut down exercises to 2 sets to keep session under 90 mins (**is 4 sets of the main exercise too
  much? - twice in a row now**)"
- "stopped at 2 sets as workout was taking a long time and **didnt want to cut out the other
  exercises**"
- "ended early - hungry"
- "ended session early - low energy"
- "only had small amount of time - cut short"
- "did lunges last due to classes being on"

`coaching-method.md` already carries the observation: **sessions overrun and end early when the main
lift is 4 sets; at 3 sets the same session finished in 66 minutes with every lift held or improved.**

## The conclusion

**The week does not lack days. The sessions lack room.** Three upper and lower sessions carry
**eight exercises each**, run to 90 minutes, and are routinely abandoned before the end - which
means the last exercises on the list are being dropped by attrition rather than by decision.

Two pieces of evidence say trimming costs less than it feels like:

- **Strength has steep diminishing returns to weekly volume** and reaches a functional plateau, more
  sharply than hypertrophy does.
- **Frequency has a more reliable effect on strength than volume does.** Training a lift more often
  beats training it harder in one sitting.

So an eight-exercise, 90-minute session that gets abandoned at exercise six is strictly worse than a
six-exercise, 60-minute session that gets finished - and probably worse than it looks, because the
abandoned exercises are always the same ones.

**A shorter session is also how a second run becomes possible without a sixth day.** That is the
link between this part and Part 6, and it is why "where does the run go" is the wrong first
question.

# Part 4 - The sequencing rules

Collected from the concurrent-training and hybrid-training evidence, in priority order.

1. **Never run hard the day before the heaviest lift.** Running-induced muscle damage impairs force
   production and raises injury risk under a loaded squat or deadlift.
2. **Keep the hardest strength work and the hardest cardio on different days.**
3. **Allow 48-72 hours between high-intensity sessions** of any kind.
4. **If both happen in one day, lift first** - resistance before endurance preserves strength
   adaptations better than the reverse - and **separate them by at least three hours** where
   possible.
5. **Easy cardio is cheap; hard cardio is not.** Easy aerobic work does not tax the nervous system
   the way heavy lifting or threshold running does, so its placement matters far less. This is the
   rule that makes a second run tractable.
6. **Flexibility work is training, not recovery.** Best 2+ days after the strength work that loads
   the same area; **worst the day after it, or parked on a rest day as "active recovery"**
   (`flexibility-method.md` section 6).
7. **Interference is not a live concern at one or two runs a week.** Three recent meta-analyses found
   concurrent training did not significantly compromise strength or hypertrophy. It becomes a concern
   at four or five runs alongside four heavy lifting days, which nobody is proposing.

# Part 5 - Where a second run could go

## The mechanism already exists

**`Weekend run (optional)` is already in the program** - `day: "Optional"`, Garmin-tracked, no
target, no progression, deliberately skippable. It was built on 11 August for exactly this purpose,
along with the whole "Optional" day concept and the "Last time" card that optional sessions open
with.

**The second run does not need building. It needs using.** That is a much smaller ask than it
appeared to be at the end of the first research pass.

## Saturday or Sunday

Both are currently empty. Under rule 5 the run must be **easy** - Wednesday keeps the quality
session - and once it is easy, both slots are viable.

| | Saturday | Sunday |
|---|---|---|
| Gap after Lower 1 (Fri) | 1 day | 2 days |
| Gap before Lower 2 (Mon) | 2 days | 1 day |
| Effect on Monday's squat | minimal | some, but it is an easy run |
| Side effect | **runs on legs already tired from Lower 1** | fresher legs, cleaner run |
| Leaves a full rest day | Sunday | Saturday |

**Saturday is the better default, and the reason is Hyrox.** Running on legs already fatigued by a
lower-body session is *compromised running*, which is the single most Hyrox-specific quality there
is (`hyrox-method.md` Phase 3). The thing that looks like a drawback is the feature. It also keeps
Monday's heavy squat clean, which rule 1 cares about more.

Sunday is the better choice if the priority is the quality of the run itself rather than its
specificity - for instance while Daniel is extending his longest run, where a tired-legs run would
cap the distance.

**This is Daniel's call, not the coach's**, and it goes through `propose_suggestion_tool`.

## Cerys's second run is probably not a run

While shins are the governor, her second session should be **incline walking**, not running -
`coaching-method.md` Part 3 is explicit that incline walking at 4-6% is how she gets aerobic work,
and `running-method.md` Part 2 explains that cross-training preserves the engine while the
return-to-run sequence builds the tissue.

**They are training together but they are not doing the same session.** The program already supports
this - `Run: Daniel` and `Run: Cerys` are separate sessions on the same day - so a per-person weekend
session would follow an established pattern rather than needing a new one.

# Part 6 - Where the flexibility session could go

Honest answer first: **in the current five-day week there is no free slot, and every option costs
something.** That is a real constraint, not an oversight, and it is why
`flexibility-method.md` section 8 has left it open.

The skills chosen are **pike and deep squat**, which load posterior chain, adductors and calves -
exactly what Lower 1 and Lower 2 load. So the rule that binds is rule 6.

| Day | Gap since a Lower day | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Lower 2 itself | Same-day option: lift morning, flexibility evening. Ranks 2nd in the method |
| Tuesday | 1 day after Lower 2 | **Worst-ranked slot.** No |
| Wednesday | 2 days after Lower 2 | Meets the best-case rule, but it is the quality run day - adds load to the one session that must be good |
| Thursday | 3 days after Lower 2 | Meets the rule comfortably. Cost: fatigue carries into Friday's Lower 1 - the session that already keeps getting dropped |
| Friday | Lower 1 itself | Same-day option, ranks 2nd |
| Saturday | 1 day after Lower 1 | **Worst-ranked slot.** No - and it is the proposed run slot |
| Sunday | 2 days after Lower 1 | Meets the best-case rule. Cost: fatigue carries into Monday's Lower 2 |

**Sunday and Thursday are the two that satisfy the method.** The choice between them is really the
choice of *which lower day you are willing to compromise* - Monday's Lower 2 or Friday's Lower 1.

Given Part 2, **Friday's Lower 1 is already the fragile session** and should not be given another
reason to be skipped. That argues for **Sunday**, with Saturday as the easy run - which happens to
fill both empty days with the two things the research says are missing.

The remaining option worth considering is the **same-day** version, ranked second in the method and
often the most practical: strength in the morning, flexibility in the evening, on a Monday or a
Friday. It works because the fatigue signal has not reached the brain yet. It needs two trips to the
gym, or floor space at home, which is a logistics question rather than a training one.

# Part 7 - The 7 September reset

Daniel's new work schedule starts on Monday 7 September. Rather than porting the current week
across, these are the questions worth answering while the week is being rebuilt:

1. **How many days, honestly?** Five is the current answer and the week executes as four when it
   slips. Four excellent sessions beat five that drift.
2. **What goes first?** Whatever matters most, because the last thing is what gets lost. If the
   deadlift is genuinely one of three headline goals, Lower 1 should not be last.
3. **How long is a session allowed to be?** Naming a number - 60 minutes, say - and building the
   sessions to fit it is a different exercise from building sessions and finding out they take 90.
4. **Do the two empty weekend days get used?** An easy run and a flexibility session is the shape the
   research points at, and both mechanisms already exist in the app.
5. **Are the sessions the same for both of them?** They already diverge on the run day. Cerys's
   Hyrox load runway (`strength-method.md` Part 5) and Daniel's barbell goals are genuinely different
   projects, and pretending otherwise costs both of them.

**None of this is decided here.** `coaching-method.md` Part 3 is explicit that program structure is
not the coach's to change; sets, reps, targets and which exercises go through
`propose_suggestion_tool`. This file is the reasoning a proposal would cite.

# Part 8 - What not to do

- **Do not add a sixth training day to fix session overrun.** The problem is length, not count, and
  adding a day to a week that already drifts makes the drift worse.
- **Do not make the second run a second quality session.** The commonest error when going from one
  run to two is making both hard, which produces the worst version of every intensity model.
- **Do not put flexibility on a rest day and call it recovery.** It is the worst-ranked option in the
  method and it is the exact mistake `flexibility-method.md` was written to stop.
- **Do not redesign the week before knowing what the new work schedule actually looks like.** It
  starts 7 September and nobody has said what it is yet.
