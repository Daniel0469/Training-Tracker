## What this does to the training week

The constraint that built the current routine was *no free days - every session is a pre-07:00
morning or a post-18:00 evening*. Against the real timetable:

| Day | On campus | What the day actually looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | no | Free. Self-study, scheduled around training rather than the other way round. |
| **Tue** | no | Free. Same. |
| **Wed** | 09:00-11:00 | The **only** early start. Home by about midday; the afternoon is clear. |
| **Thu** | 14:00-16:00 | **Morning free at home.** Leave about 13:00, home about 17:00. |
| **Fri** | 14:00-16:00 | Same as Thursday. |

Daniel, 14 September, on Monday and Tuesday: *"open depending on the work I have - but that can all
be scheduled around"*. So the two empty days are **schedulable**, not merely empty: study flexes
around training on those days, not the reverse.

Three things the old assumption forced that the timetable does not:

1. **The 40-45 minute morning cap on the Tuesday and Thursday runs.** Both were sized by the 07:50
   train. Neither day has a train. The cap is the stated reason Thursday's quality run was cut from
   5 x 6:00 to 4 x 4:00, and the reason Tuesday carries the duration progression toward the 8 km gate
   on its own.
2. **The 05:30 wakes on Tuesday and Thursday**, and the 22:00 bedtime written as *"a requirement, not
   a hope"*. Wednesday is now the only day that needs either.
3. **The 75-90 minute evening ceiling** set by getting home at 18:00. Monday has no commute; Thursday
   and Friday get home nearer 17:00.

What the timetable does **not** touch is which day each session sits on. That reasoning - both runs
paired with upper-body evenings, the quality run three days clear of Monday's squats, the easy run
twelve hours after them - is about spacing between sessions, not about uni. It stands unchanged.
