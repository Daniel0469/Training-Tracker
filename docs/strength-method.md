# Strength method - progression, and what the goals actually cost

**Written 2026-09-02**, the same day it was parked. Daniel asked for it back in the second research
pass.

Two jobs, and they are not the same job:

- **Daniel's stated goals:** 100 kg bench, 200 kg squat, 200 kg deadlift. Long-horizon, absolute
  numbers, no deadline.
- **Cerys's two:** her first unassisted pull-up, and enough lower-body and posterior-chain strength
  to handle **men's Open mixed-doubles loads** at Hyrox - a 152 kg sled, 2 x 24 kg carries, a 20 kg
  sandbag. That one has a deadline attached the moment a race is booked.

There is a third thing this file has to do, which is tell the truth about timescales.

# Part 1 - Where they actually are, and the thing nobody had noticed

## The numbers

**Daniel, squat:** 40 (25 Jun) → 48 (4 Jul) → 70 (27 Jul) → 80 (11 Aug) → **100 (18 Aug)**. That is
**+60 kg in eight weeks.**

**Daniel, bench:** 20 (23 Jul) → **50 (12 Aug)** → 50 (19 Aug). +30 kg in three weeks.

**Daniel, deadlift: one entry in the entire log.** 80 kg, 28 Aug.

**Cerys, squat:** 12 (25 Jun) → 16 (4 Jul) → 20 (27 Jul) → 20 (11 Aug) → **30 (18 Aug)**. +18 kg in
eight weeks.

**Cerys, deadlift: also one entry.** 30 kg, 28 Aug, the same session as Daniel's.

**Cerys, pull-up:** 47.8 kg effective and rising, assist coming down 36 → 32.

## The thing nobody had noticed: they are novices, and that is good news

`coaching-method.md` carries this observation, dated 2026-08-20:

> "Responds to load faster than an incremental model predicts. Three times running he has overtaken
> a prescribed progression and it landed."

**That observation is correct about the behaviour and wrong about the cause.** It reads as though
Daniel is unusual. He is not - he is a novice in the steepest part of the linear-progression curve,
which is exactly where everyone adds weight faster than an incremental model predicts. Published
novice expectations are **+40-100 kg on the squat in a year**. Daniel did +60 kg in eight weeks.

Two consequences, and both matter more than the correction itself:

1. **The practical advice stays exactly the same.** Give him a target and a ceiling and let him pick
   inside it. That is the right way to programme a novice whose true capacity is ahead of any number
   you could prescribe. Nothing about the current approach needs changing.
2. **It will stop, and probably soon.** +60 kg in eight weeks annualises to roughly +390 kg, which is
   not a thing that happens. Some of that curve is genuine adaptation and some is learning the
   movement and finding out what he can hold onto. **When it stops it will look like failure and it
   will not be** - it is the expected end of the novice phase, and having a plan for it is most of
   what this document is for. See Part 3.

Cerys is on the same curve, less steep, and the same logic applies.

## The deadlift is not weak, it is untrained

Daniel's deadlift is 80 kg against a 100 kg squat, which is **80% of his squat**. The population
picture is the other way round: the deadlift typically sits at **110-125% of the squat**, and the
median intermediate male squats about 86% of what he deadlifts.

The obvious reading is a technique weak point or a posterior-chain deficit. **The data does not
support that reading, because there is only one deadlift session on record, ever.** You cannot
diagnose a lift from n=1, and his own note on the day says what it was:

> "only deadlift - focused on form and getting the right weight for next session"

That is a deliberate feeler session, not a ceiling.

**And the reason there is only one has nothing to do with the deadlift at all.** It is programmed -
first exercise on Lower 1 - but Lower 1 is the **Friday** session, the last in the week, and the week
runs about a day late and slips. Lower 1 has been logged **twice since 20 July**. `week-method.md`
Part 2 has the full evidence, and the principle it produces is the important bit:

> Whatever sits last in the week is the thing you will lose.

So the deadlift is currently sitting in the slot reserved for the session you are willing to drop,
while being one of three named strength goals. **That is a scheduling problem with a scheduling fix,
and it will do more for his deadlift than any programming change in this document.**

The same single session gave Cerys her only deadlift entry, 30 kg, and the same explanation applies.

**What follows: the deadlift is the lift with the most headroom and the least information.** Train
it a few times before concluding anything about it.

# Part 2 - What the goals actually cost

This section exists because "100 kg bench, 200 kg squat, 200 kg deadlift" reads as one goal of
roughly equal parts, and it is not.

## Relative to bodyweight, at 74.6 kg

| Goal | Multiple of bodyweight | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| 100 kg bench | **1.34x** | Solid intermediate |
| 200 kg squat | **2.68x** | Advanced, edging elite |
| 200 kg deadlift | **2.68x** | Advanced |

**This is the opposite of what most people assume.** The bench is the number that sounds hardest -
it is the slowest-progressing lift and 100 kg is a landmark - but relative to a 74.6 kg bodyweight
it is by far the most modest of the three. **The 200 kg squat is the hard one**, and a 200/200 squat
and deadlift is an unusual pairing given that most lifters deadlift more than they squat.

## The timescale, honestly

Published annual gains, by training phase:

| Phase | Squat | Bench | Deadlift |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Novice** | +40 to 100+ kg | +20 to 40+ kg | +40 to 120+ kg |
| **Intermediate** | +10 to 40 kg | +5 to 20 kg | +10 to 50 kg |
| **Advanced** | +0 to 10 kg | +0 to 7 kg | +0 to 15 kg |

Beginners add weight roughly weekly, intermediates monthly, advanced lifters quarterly at best.

**Daniel is at 100 / 50 / 80 and needs 200 / 100 / 200.** If the rest of the novice year delivers at
the top of its range he could plausibly reach something like 180 / 85 / 175 - genuinely close - and
then every subsequent kilogram gets much more expensive. So:

- **These are multi-year goals**, most likely three to five years of consistent training, not one.
- **The next twelve months will deliver more of the distance than any twelve months after it.** That
  is an argument for consistency now rather than for optimisation now.
- **Falling bodyweight makes all three harder in absolute terms.** Daniel went 78.4 to 74.6 kg over
  five weeks while chasing three absolute-load goals. That is not a criticism, it is an interaction
  nobody has looked at - see `fuelling-method.md` Part 1.

None of that is a reason to change the goals. It is a reason for the coaching not to imply they are
twelve months away, and for progress to be judged against a multi-year curve rather than against
the last eight weeks.

# Part 3 - Progression models, and what to do when linear stops

## The continuum

Periodisation is best understood as a continuum indexed by **training age**, not as competing
religions:

- **Novice: linear progression.** Add weight every session or every week. It works because recovery
  outpaces adaptation at this stage, and nothing more complicated beats it. **Both of them are here.**
- **Intermediate: undulating (DUP).** Vary intensity and volume within the week rather than across
  months. In trained individuals, undulating produced **23.7-24.5% strength gains against 17.0-18.0%
  for linear** in one comparison, roughly 28% faster. Other work finds the two similar. The honest
  summary: **linear tends to win for novices, undulating tends to win for trained lifters**, and the
  difference is real but not enormous.
- **Advanced: block periodisation**, saturating one quality at a time.

**Do not move Daniel or Cerys off linear progression while it is still working.** Complexity added
early costs progress and buys nothing. The trigger for changing is in the next section.

## The signs that linear progression has ended

Not one bad session. The pattern:

- **The same weight fails on two or three consecutive attempts**, with sleep and food normal.
- **The prescribed jump stops landing** - the opposite of Daniel's current pattern of overtaking it.
- **Session quality degrades late in the week** rather than at a single lift.
- Progress continues on some lifts and stalls on one. **That is a lift problem, not a programme
  problem** - fix that lift.

When it happens, the first moves in order: **check sleep and food before changing the programme**
(`recovery-method.md`, `fuelling-method.md` - both are more likely culprits at this stage than
programming), then reduce the size of the jump, then add a lighter day, then move to undulating.

## Volume and frequency

- **Both hypertrophy and strength improve with more weekly sets, with diminishing returns - and
  strength's diminishing returns are steeper**, reaching a functional plateau while hypertrophy
  keeps creeping up.
- **Frequency has a more reliable effect on strength than on hypertrophy.** If a lift needs to move,
  training it more often is better supported than training it harder in one session.
- **Direct and indirect sets are not equal.** The best-predicting method counts an indirect set as
  half a set.

**Applied:** Daniel's deadlift has been trained once. That is a frequency problem with a frequency
solution, and it is more likely to move his deadlift than anything else in this document.

## Autoregulation, and the data already sitting in the app

The evidence: **autoregulated load prescription is at least as good as percentage-based, and RIR-based
autoregulation produced greater strength improvements than fixed loading over 12 weeks** in one
trial. Subjective (RIR/RPE) and objective (velocity-based) approaches perform similarly. Autoregulated
sessions also preserve bar speed better than fixed prescriptions.

**The app already collects RPE on every set.** It was added in July, it covers lifting and running,
and **nothing currently reads it.** That is the single largest piece of unused information in the
project:

- RPE per set is exactly the input an autoregulated progression needs.
- It would let a prescription say "work up to a top set at RPE 8" rather than naming a number that is
  either too light or too heavy depending on the day - which is precisely the problem Daniel keeps
  solving by overtaking the prescribed number.
- **It is also the honest fix for the observation in Part 1.** "Give him a target and a ceiling" is
  autoregulation done manually. The data to do it properly is already there.

This is a proposal, not a coaching note. It is on the backlog.

# Part 4 - How much strength does Hyrox actually need?

The answer is genuinely surprising and it should stop anyone over-rotating on this.

In the peer-reviewed Hyrox study (11 recreational athletes, simulated Open division), finish time
correlated with:

| Predicted a faster time | |
|---|---|
| VO2max | ρ = -0.71, p = 0.01 |
| Endurance training volume | ρ = -0.68, p = 0.04 |
| Lower body fat % | ρ = 0.67, p = 0.03 |

| Did **not** predict finish time | |
|---|---|
| Hand grip strength | p = 0.79 |
| Resistance training volume | p = 0.31 |
| Muscle mass % | p = 0.94 |

Correlations appeared **only for the runs, not for the exercise stations.** The authors describe
Hyrox as an endurance-focused modality with **moderate to low maximum-strength requirements**.

**Caveat, stated plainly: n = 11.** That is a small sample and these are correlations, not causes.
Treat the direction as informative and the precision as not.

## What follows from it

**Strength for Hyrox is a sufficiency problem, not a maximisation problem.** You need enough to move
the sled and hold the handles; past that, more strength does not appear to buy finish time, and the
training hours are better spent on the engine.

That has two consequences here, pulling in opposite directions:

1. **Daniel does not need to get stronger for Hyrox.** At a 100 kg squat and an 80 kg deadlift he is
   already comfortably inside every mixed-doubles load. His barbell goals are worth pursuing for
   their own sake - and they help his running economy (`running-method.md` Part 4) - but they are
   **not** Hyrox preparation and should not be sold as such.
2. **Cerys does need to get stronger for Hyrox**, because she is plausibly below sufficiency on the
   sled and the carries at a 30 kg squat. Her work is about **crossing a threshold**, not about
   maximising a number, and it can stop climbing once the stations are manageable.

**And for both of them, the study says the same thing the arithmetic in `hyrox-method.md` said: the
engine is what wins the race.** It is worth noticing that a blog-derived time-split calculation and
a peer-reviewed physiological study landed on the same conclusion by completely different routes.

# Part 5 - Cerys's two strength jobs

## The pull-up

Covered fully in `female-athlete-method.md` Part 4. Summary: 47.8 kg effective and rising, it is her
only stated strength goal, it needs neither shins nor hips, **eccentrics are the highest-yield
addition**, and it transfers to the Hyrox sled pull. When a lower-body injury flares, this is the
opportunity, not the lost week.

## The Hyrox load runway

The stations that need her below-the-bar strength to come up:

| Station | Load | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Sled push | 152 kg | Leg drive, horizontal force, calf and quad endurance |
| Sled pull | 103 kg | Posterior chain, grip, hand-over-hand rhythm |
| Farmers carry | 2 x 24 kg | **Grip endurance**, trunk, posture |
| Sandbag lunges | 20 kg | Unilateral leg strength, hip-flexor length on the trailing leg |

Three notes:

- **Grip appears twice**, and the two stations sit close in the running order. Farmers walks at about
  50% bodyweight per hand, 3 x 40 m at the end of an upper-body session, is the standard
  prescription and it costs almost nothing to add.
- **Her 70 kg standing calf raise and 50 kg hip abduction are already respectable.** The raw strength
  in some of the relevant muscles is there; what may not be is the endurance and the control under
  fatigue, which are different qualities.
- **The doubles split is the safety valve.** Daniel can take the majority of the sled metres
  (`hyrox-method.md` Part 2), so this is about her being able to contribute rather than about her
  matching him.

**Start it early and gently.** It is the longest strength runway on the list and the one thing here
that a race date would make urgent.

# Part 6 - What this file does not decide

- **No sets, reps, loads or exercises are prescribed here.** `coaching-method.md` Part 3 is explicit
  that program structure is not the coach's to change - sets, reps, targets and which exercises all
  go through `propose_suggestion_tool` for Daniel to approve. This file is the reasoning that a
  proposal would cite, not the proposal.
- **Nothing here says the current programme is wrong.** Linear progression is the right model for
  where both of them are, and it is working. The value of this document is in knowing what to do when
  it stops, and in not mistaking a normal novice curve for something special.
- **The deadlift question stays open** until it has been trained more than once.
